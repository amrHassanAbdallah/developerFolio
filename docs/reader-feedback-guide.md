# Validate the portfolio with real readers

Status: prepared for the owner to use. No reader-test invitations have been sent and no usability feedback has been collected. The website now includes existing freelance feedback from a user-supplied Upwork screenshot and an indexed Fiverr review; see client-feedback-sources.md. These testimonials are not results of the reader test below.

## Who to invite

Invite one recruiter who hires backend engineers and one engineering lead or potential client who evaluates backend work. Share the deployed site, or show the local preview in a screen share. A localhost URL will not work on their computers.

## First 30 seconds

Show the homepage without explaining the intended message. After 30 seconds, hide it and ask:

1. What kind of work does Amr do?
2. What problem would you consider contacting him about?
3. What specific experience or result do you remember?

Record their own words. Do not suggest the expected answer or correct them during this step.

## Two-minute task

For the recruiter: “Find his current role, a concrete example of technical ownership, and his résumé.”

For the engineering lead/client: “You need an external platform integrated into an existing backend. Find evidence that he has done relevant work, then find how to contact him.”

Observe where they click, whether they find the case study, and whether they use the evidence links. Ask:

- What made you more or less confident?
- Which wording was unclear or sounded unsupported?
- What information would you still need before starting a conversation?
- What, if anything, felt like unnecessary detail?

## Optional testimonial request

Choose a former manager or collaborator who directly observed the work. The owner can send this draft:

> I’m updating my engineering portfolio. Would you be comfortable sharing two or three sentences about work we did together—particularly my ownership, technical judgment, or delivery? A concrete example would be useful. If you’re happy for me to publish it, please confirm the wording and the name, role, and company attribution you want shown. There’s no obligation.

Publish only the actual approved wording and attribution. Get approval for edits, including shortening. Do not imply their employer endorses Amr unless that is explicitly authorized. Keep this workflow in these notes; do not add placeholder testimonials or “coming soon” sections to the website.
